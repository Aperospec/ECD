# Visual Concept Skill

## Purpose

Visual Concept decides what visual relationship, world, metaphor, evidence behavior, or formal logic will make the approved idea perceptible.

It does not design every page, choose final Production settings, use style labels as concepts, or treat a reference as an imitation target.

Apply `core/REFERENCE_CONTRACT.md` and the project's Evidence Obligation.

## Inputs

- Greenlit Treatment;
- Script-Aligned Creative Script;
- Frame Scripts and Written Visual Requirements;
- exact copy and language variants;
- Evidence Obligation and public claim ceiling;
- Active Visual input projections;
- Reference Records, original ECD intent, assigned roles, rights, and prohibited transfer;
- source evidence and reconstruction / simulation conditions;
- target surface and viewing conditions;
- Art Director assignment.

## Method

### 1. Reconstruct the visual problem

State:

- what the audience must notice first;
- what relationship or change must become visible;
- what must remain literal, evidentiary, or identifiable;
- what may be metaphorical;
- what reality or validation status the image may imply;
- what misreading is most dangerous;
- what later design disciplines need from the concept.

### 2. Read material references through the shared contract

For each reference preserve:

- original ECD wording or faithful summary;
- assigned role and authority class;
- source, owner, date, version, rights, attribution, and evidence context;
- active and Deferred stage roles.

Then perform four separate passes:

1. **Description** — observable subjects, structures, masses, light, material, sequence, and viewer relation;
2. **Formal Analysis** — hierarchy, framing, scale, depth, line, value, color, rhythm, density, type–image, and evidence behavior;
3. **Interpretation** — perception, emotion, meaning, or behavior produced;
4. **Controlled Transfer** — transferable principles, authorized direct-use content, open attributes, source-specific expression excluded, prohibited use, and alignment questions.

Do not collapse these passes into a vague style summary.

### 3. Generate materially distinct routes

Routes may differ in:

- governing visual thesis;
- viewer relationship;
- world / spatial logic;
- literal versus metaphorical treatment;
- type-led versus image-led authority;
- evidence behavior;
- scale and distance;
- sequence grammar;
- emotional temperature and tension;
- how activated reference principles are recombined into an original system.

Palette-only or font-only variations do not count.

### 4. Make low-cost proof

Use sketches, diagrams, rough collages, written spatial propositions, or exploratory image studies only to answer the route's central question.

Mark exploratory imagery clearly. It is not a final page, evidence, or Production-intent asset.

Visual realism may not imply a stronger factual or validation status than the Evidence Obligation permits.

### 5. Compare routes

Evaluate:

- fidelity to Treatment and Script;
- specificity to this project;
- clarity at social viewing conditions;
- capacity for exact copy and evidence;
- sequence potential;
- image and typography opportunity;
- fidelity to assigned reference roles;
- originality and similarity risk;
- risk of cliché, imitation, semantic substitution, or false evidence;
- feasibility without allowing feasibility to dictate the idea.

### 6. Recommend one route

State:

- selected visual thesis;
- why alternatives are weaker;
- which reference principles transfer and which source-specific elements remain excluded;
- what is fixed;
- what remains open to Storyboard, Editorial Design, Typography, and Image Direction;
- what proof is required next;
- any ECD decision or rights blocker.

## Specificity and independence tests

Ask:

`If the title, hero image, and colors were replaced with unrelated content, would the same concept still work almost unchanged?`

If yes, the route is probably a style template rather than a project-specific visual concept.

Also ask:

`If the references were removed, could the selected visual thesis still be explained as a solution to this project's own communication problem?`

If no, the route may be imitation rather than controlled transfer.

## Self-check

- Are routes materially different?
- Is each route a communicative idea rather than a mood board?
- Were observation, interpretation, transfer, and ECD authority kept separate?
- Are assigned reference roles and prohibited uses explicit?
- Does the recommendation preserve exact content, evidence, and reality position?
- Does it leave page design, typography, and image craft to their own Skills?
- Is exploratory work clearly separated from Production intent?

## Failure routing

- visual problem originates in weak Script or Frame Script → Editorial through Art Director;
- exact copy, language variant, or evidence changes the route materially → Art Director / Producer;
- reference role or original ECD intent is ambiguous → Producer / Reference Contract;
- third-party transfer exceeds rights boundary → Development Claims Rights;
- route implies unsupported fact or validation → Development / Editorial;
- route cannot support target surface → reconsider route before page design.
