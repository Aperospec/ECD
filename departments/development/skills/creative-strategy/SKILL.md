# Creative Strategy Skill

## Purpose

Creative Strategy decides what is worth making before deciding how to express it.

It does not write the Creative Treatment, final copy, page sequence, or visual design.

## Inputs

- original request and intended use;
- available sources and references;
- audience, account, platform, and existing-content context;
- evidence, rights, time, and scope constraints;
- Producer and Development Director assignment.

## Method

### 1. Reconstruct the real request

Separate:

- what the user literally asked;
- the communication outcome they appear to seek;
- constraints they explicitly set;
- assumptions that still require verification.

### 2. Map the available material

Identify:

- strongest source idea;
- strongest proof or visual evidence;
- novelty relative to existing work;
- audience curiosity, benefit, tension, or practical value;
- what is overfamiliar, weak, derivative, or unsuitable.

### 3. Generate strategic options

When the angle is open, create materially different options based on:

- different audience question;
- different proposition;
- different account role;
- different source emphasis;
- different practical or emotional payoff.

Do not create cosmetic title variants and call them strategies.

### 4. Evaluate options

Judge:

- relevance to intended audience;
- fit with the account or project;
- distinctiveness from recent content;
- strength of source and evidence;
- capacity for a clear creative mechanism;
- rights and claim risk;
- visual and editorial potential without prematurely designing them;
- likelihood of becoming generic content.

### 5. Recommend one direction

State why it is stronger than plausible alternatives and what must remain true in later development.

## Self-check

- Is the recommendation more than a paraphrase of the source?
- Is there one clear audience reason to care?
- Is the angle distinct from existing work?
- Are novelty and trend claims supported rather than assumed?
- Have downstream copy and visual decisions remained open?

## Failure routing

- missing or unreliable source facts → Research Verification;
- third-party use or attribution concern → Claims Rights;
- no viable angle after comparison → Development Director;
- user objective or scope conflict → Creative Producer.
