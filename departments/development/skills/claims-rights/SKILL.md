# Claims and Rights Review Skill

## Purpose

This Skill protects the project's factual position, speaker position, attribution, and use of third-party material.

It does not provide legal advice or invent permission. It identifies the safest supportable creative use and any unresolved risk that must reach Producer or ECD.

## Inputs

- source materials and provenance;
- proposed concept or Treatment;
- intended public claims;
- quotations, screenshots, images, names, brands, likenesses, or demonstrations;
- platform and publication context;
- Research Verification Return;
- Development Director assignment.

## Method

### 1. Classify each source use

- inspiration only;
- factual reference;
- quotation;
- screenshot or reproduction;
- transformation;
- comparison;
- demonstration;
- parody or commentary;
- creator attribution;
- implied endorsement or affiliation risk.

### 2. Classify the project's reality position

- documented fact;
- personal real experience;
- reconstruction from memory;
- hypothetical example;
- fictional scene;
- concept visualization;
- speculative future;
- product demonstration;
- simulation.

The main narrative must signal the correct position. A late disclaimer cannot repair a body written to make a fictional or hypothetical event appear factual.

### 3. Review public claims

Check whether wording implies:

- a product performed something it did not perform;
- a creator endorses the work;
- generated imagery is documentary evidence;
- a concept visualization is an actual workflow or result;
- a future possibility is currently available;
- a source's example is the user's own result.

### 4. Set use and attribution conditions

Record:

- what may be quoted or paraphrased;
- what should be linked or attributed;
- what should not be reproduced;
- what brand, username, watermark, or identity treatment is appropriate;
- what transformation boundary applies;
- what permission remains unknown.

### 5. Draft boundary language

Create concise language for the Treatment and later public copy. The boundary should be clear before the audience is misled, not hidden at the end.

## Self-check

- Is the project's real / fictional / conceptual position honest in the main communication?
- Are product and AI capabilities described no more strongly than verified?
- Is third-party work distinguishable from the user's output?
- Are attribution and transformation conditions usable downstream?
- Does any unresolved issue require ECD or legal review?

## Failure routing

- uncertain factual basis → Research Verification;
- boundary weakens or changes the concept → Concept Development;
- user intends a higher-risk use → Creative Producer / ECD decision;
- permission cannot be established → blocker or alternative-source recommendation.
