# Concept Development Skill

## Purpose

Concept Development converts a strategic opportunity into a coherent idea that later departments can faithfully elaborate.

A concept is not a topic, title, style, object list, or page outline. It is a specific proposition joined to a governing relationship, mechanism, transformation, contrast, or experience.

## Inputs

- Creative Strategy Return;
- verified source and research findings;
- claims / rights boundaries;
- audience and platform conditions;
- user constraints and feedback;
- Development Director assignment.

## Method

### 1. State the raw proposition

Complete:

`The work asks the audience to see ______ differently because ______.`

### 2. Identify the governing logic

Determine what makes the idea hold together, for example:

- a transformation;
- a contradiction;
- a relationship;
- a hidden mechanism;
- a comparison;
- a speculative premise;
- a procedural chain;
- a visual or verbal metaphor.

Do not select a mechanism merely because it is dramatic.

### 3. Develop alternatives

When the concept is not already fixed, create at least two materially different concept routes. They must differ in proposition or governing logic, not only title or tone.

### 4. Stress-test each route

Ask:

- Does it still work without fashionable wording?
- Does it require false factual framing?
- Is the source evidence sufficient?
- Can it become a complete work without padding?
- Could examples replace the concept accidentally?
- Could downstream teams misread it into a nearby project?
- Does it create an honest and useful audience consequence?

### 5. Select and write the Treatment

The complete Treatment should explain:

- one-sentence creative core;
- project premise;
- governing logic;
- audience entry and payoff;
- source and reality basis;
- conceptual, speculative, demonstrative, or metaphorical status;
- boundaries;
- why rejected routes are weaker;
- what Editorial and Visual must not decide yet.

Write it as a clear creative narrative, not a form filled with slogans.

## Self-check

- Is the core one intelligible idea?
- Is there a real governing logic?
- Is the work specific rather than a generic theme?
- Are fact, imagination, metaphor, and demonstration honest?
- Could Editorial create a Script without inventing the project?
- Have page structure, exact copy, palette, typography, layout, and image style remained open?

## Failure routing

- weak or generic opportunity → Creative Strategy;
- evidence or factual uncertainty → Research Verification;
- claim or rights boundary → Claims Rights;
- no route survives stress test → Development Director.
