# Research Verification Skill

## Purpose

Research Verification establishes what can responsibly support the project at the depth required by `core/EVIDENCE_OBLIGATION.md`.

It does not choose the final creative angle, write promotional claims, use a source merely because it is vivid, or imply practical validation that did not occur.

## Inputs

- research question and intended downstream use;
- Evidence Obligation Record and claim-level overrides;
- source links, posts, files, screenshots, products, repositories, papers, or datasets;
- Reference Records and assigned roles;
- current strategic or concept options;
- date, version, region, sample, and availability sensitivity;
- evidence, quotation, attribution, validation, and commercial-use requirements;
- Development Director assignment.

## Method

### 1. Define the decision the research serves

Record:

- what must be known;
- why it affects the project;
- Evidence Obligation mode;
- required certainty or reproducibility;
- what would change if the answer differs;
- what evidence is unnecessary for the actual promise.

Do not research broadly without a decision consequence.

### 2. Inspect primary material

Prefer the original post, product documentation, repository, research paper, dataset, official statement, creator explanation, or direct artifact where available.

Record source identity, author, date, version, region, context, and access limitations.

Use the active roles in `core/REFERENCE_CONTRACT.md`. Do not silently turn a mood or concept reference into factual evidence.

### 3. Separate evidence classes

For every material statement classify:

- independently verified fact;
- source's own claim;
- directly observed demonstration;
- practical result observed by the studio;
- reasonable inference;
- interpretation;
- speculation;
- unknown or disputed;
- temporally unstable fact requiring current verification.

A demonstration is not automatically a reliable product capability. A source claim is not an observed studio result.

### 4. Check context, contradiction, and limits

Inspect whether:

- a quotation is truncated;
- a demonstration is mistaken for a general capability;
- a concept example is mistaken for an actual result;
- a source date or version makes the claim stale;
- another reliable source materially disagrees;
- a screenshot omits qualifying context;
- region, access tier, hardware, account, or environment limits availability;
- sample size or selection bias weakens the inference.

### 5. Apply mode-specific depth

#### Speculative / Conceptual

- verify only factual premises that materially affect meaning;
- do not demand current implementation proof for a clearly conceptual proposition;
- preserve the boundary between inspiration, imagination, and present fact.

#### Evidence-based Editorial

- verify the key facts the public explanation relies on;
- preserve source context and material limitations;
- provide claim-ready wording no stronger than the evidence;
- do not pretend to have completed product validation.

#### Product / Commercial Validation

When tools, access, time, and authority permit, validate:

- setup and execution;
- representative inputs;
- reproducibility across more than one trial where the promise implies repeatability;
- failure cases and recovery conditions;
- environment, dependency, version, region, access, cost, and operational requirements;
- license, redistribution, commercial-use, privacy, and security constraints relevant to the offer;
- difference between source claims and observed studio results.

When practical validation cannot be performed, state exactly what remains unvalidated and narrow the public promise. Do not substitute a creator example or one successful output for reliability proof.

### 6. Build a practical validation protocol when required

Record before testing:

```markdown
Capability or promise:
Environment:
Dependencies:
Representative inputs:
Success criteria:
Failure criteria:
Number and type of trials justified by the promise:
Measurements or observations:
Privacy / rights / cost constraints:
```

After testing, preserve both successes and failures. Do not discard inconvenient outputs.

### 7. Create claim-ready language

Provide:

- strongest accurate wording;
- narrower alternative where useful;
- required qualifier, date, version, region, sample, or disclosure;
- wording prohibited until further evidence exists.

Do not overstate certainty for dramatic or commercial effect.

### 8. Record unresolved limits

State what could not be accessed, reproduced, verified, or generalized, and how that limits the Treatment, public copy, offer, or recommendation.

## Self-check

- Did the work inspect the relevant original source where possible?
- Are facts, source claims, demonstrations, studio results, inference, and speculation separate?
- Are current facts actually current?
- Does research depth match the Evidence Obligation?
- Was practical validation real, representative, and honestly bounded when claimed?
- Is proposed wording no stronger than the evidence?
- Did research inform rather than replace creative judgment?
- Are active reference roles and prohibited uses respected?

## Failure routing

- permission, copyright, quotation, transformation, likeness, or commercial-use issue → Claims Rights;
- research undermines the proposed opportunity → Creative Strategy;
- research changes the concept's premise → Concept Development;
- validation requires unavailable tools, access, environment, budget, or authority → Development Director / Producer blocker or narrower claim;
- source cannot be accessed or verified → Development Director / Producer blocker;
- public copy later exceeds the verified ceiling → Copywriting / Copy Editing through Editorial Director.
