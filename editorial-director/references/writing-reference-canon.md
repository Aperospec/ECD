# Writing Reference Canon

## Purpose

This canon supplies method sources for ECD's internal Copy Desk.

Books and standards are treated as **compressed craft systems**, not as styles to imitate and not as text to reproduce. The studio extracts operational principles, tests them against the current audience and platform, and writes original copy in the required language and locale.

The canon is used through:

- `copy-desk-workflow.md`;
- `chinese-copy-craft.md`;
- `english-copy-craft.md`;
- `bilingual-transcreation.md`;
- `copy-quality-gate.md`.

## Source Integrity

Do not copy passages, examples, signature phrases, or an author's recognizable style.

Do not instruct the model to "write like" a living or named author.

Use each source only for generalized craft principles such as clarity, sentence structure, revision, rhetoric, reader orientation, rhythm, and concept-led copy.

When sources disagree, prioritize in this order:

1. fidelity to the Greenlit Treatment;
2. grammatical and semantic correctness;
3. idiomatic language for the target locale;
4. reader comprehension under the intended viewing condition;
5. voice, rhythm, memorability, and rhetorical effect.

A clever line that violates an earlier priority fails.

## Chinese Core Canon

### 叶圣陶《怎样写作》

Operational contribution:

- begin from something the writer actually means;
- write for communication rather than display;
- make the sentence intelligible before making it ornamental;
- judge prose from the reader's position;
- treat revision as part of writing rather than cleanup after writing;
- prefer natural modern Chinese over inflated literary posture.

Use for:

- reader orientation;
- plain but exact explanation;
- distinguishing lived meaning from empty verbal decoration;
- revision of prose that is technically grammatical but still does not feel complete.

### 吕叔湘、朱德熙《语法修辞讲话》

Operational contribution:

- diagnose the sentence through vocabulary, function words, structure, expression, and punctuation;
- check whether words belong together naturally;
- inspect word order, omitted subjects, reference, conjunctions, and clause relations;
- distinguish grammatical possibility from effective expression;
- correct the sentence at the earliest linguistic level where the defect appears.

Use for:

- awkward collocation;
- unclear pronouns or omitted subjects;
- category mismatch between subject and complement;
- malformed coordination and parallelism;
- punctuation that hides an incomplete sentence.

### 王鼎钧《作文七巧》

Operational contribution:

- choose a mode of development appropriate to the material;
- understand that narration, description, reasoning, induction, deduction, and synthesis create different reader experiences;
- transform ordinary material through selection, order, contrast, and detail;
- do not mistake a topic list for progression.

Use for:

- turning semantic propositions into scenes, movement, or argument;
- varying how adjacent pages advance;
- avoiding a carousel in which every page uses the same explanatory move.

### 陈望道《修辞学发凡》

Operational contribution:

- separate basic correctness and clarity from expressive rhetoric;
- choose rhetorical devices according to purpose, context, audience, and emotional temperature;
- treat rhetoric as a relationship among meaning, situation, and language rather than as decoration;
- avoid using metaphor, antithesis, repetition, or parallelism merely because the form sounds polished.

Use for:

- metaphor discipline;
- controlled contrast;
- deciding whether repetition is meaningful or mechanical;
- preventing pseudo-literary language from overwhelming the idea.

### GB/T 15834—2011《标点符号用法》

Operational contribution:

- use the current national standard as the baseline for Simplified Chinese punctuation;
- distinguish punctuation that marks syntax, tone, quotation, range, and hierarchy;
- do not use punctuation to simulate rhythm while leaving the underlying sentence incomplete.

Use for:

- Simplified Chinese punctuation QA;
- Chinese–English mixed composition;
- quotation marks, book-title marks, dashes, ellipses, colons, semicolons, and list structures.

## English Core Canon

### William Zinsser, *On Writing Well*

Operational contribution:

- clarity, simplicity, humanity, and revision;
- remove clutter that does not change meaning;
- prefer a recognizable human voice over institutional prose;
- preserve warmth and personality without sacrificing precision.

Use for:

- nonfiction clarity;
- cutting throat-clearing and redundant qualifiers;
- revising body copy that sounds like a strategy document.

### Steven Pinker, *The Sense of Style*

Operational contribution:

- write from the reader's perspective rather than the writer's private knowledge;
- make agents, actions, and relationships visible;
- build coherence across sentences and paragraphs;
- avoid the curse of knowledge and abstract noun chains;
- use classic, concrete explanation when the idea itself is difficult.

Use for:

- explanatory copy;
- causal clarity;
- diagnosing hidden assumptions;
- making complex concepts legible without flattening them.

### Verlyn Klinkenborg, *Several Short Sentences About Writing*

Operational contribution:

- treat the sentence as a deliberate unit;
- judge what each sentence actually says rather than what the writer intended it to say;
- build paragraphs from sentences that can stand under scrutiny;
- listen for cadence, pressure, and unnecessary continuation.

Use for:

- sentence-by-sentence editing;
- rhythm and line-break decisions;
- detecting fragments that pretend to be stylish but do not carry a complete thought.

### Roy Peter Clark, *Writing Tools*

Operational contribution:

- place strong words where readers feel them;
- vary sentence length deliberately;
- prefer strong verbs and visible action;
- control emphasis through order, punctuation, detail, and repetition;
- connect sentence craft to larger narrative and explanatory architecture.

Use for:

- headline and payoff placement;
- sentence rhythm;
- cross-page variation;
- turning abstract explanation into readable movement.

### Benjamin Dreyer, *Dreyer's English*

Operational contribution:

- copyediting discipline for grammar, usage, punctuation, consistency, and word choice;
- distinguish real errors from arbitrary prohibitions;
- edit for clarity and idiomatic modern English rather than superstition;
- apply a consistent house decision when several forms are acceptable.

Use for:

- final English copyediting;
- punctuation and usage questions;
- consistency of capitalization, compounds, numbers, and mechanics.

## Advertising and Social Copy Canon

### Luke Sullivan, *Hey, Whipple, Squeeze This*

Operational contribution:

- begin with the idea and audience tension, not decorative wording;
- make the headline, body copy, and visual work together;
- avoid saying what the image already says;
- cut generic advertising language;
- make every line earn attention.

### D&AD, *The Copy Book*

Operational contribution:

- study copy as an applied relationship among idea, audience, medium, and execution;
- favor a precise concept over a pile of claims;
- recognize that simple language can carry more force when the thinking is specific;
- use strong examples as benchmarks for decision quality, not as templates to imitate.

Use both for:

- cover lines;
- campaign and social-editorial titles;
- interaction between copy and image;
- deciding whether a line is merely correct or genuinely worth reading.

## Supporting Editorial Standards

Use plain-language guidance as a baseline when clarity is more important than literary effect:

- write with the reader's task or need in mind;
- use words the audience understands;
- prefer one clear idea per sentence when complexity does not require more;
- phrase points positively where possible;
- remove words that do not alter meaning;
- preserve nuance rather than using "plain language" as permission to oversimplify.

## Canon-to-Workflow Map

| Craft problem | Primary source layer | Required action |
|---|---|---|
| Chinese sentence feels uneducated or unfinished | 叶圣陶 +《语法修辞讲话》 | diagnose syntax, collocation, reference, and reader comprehension before polishing |
| Chinese prose is correct but dull or generic |《作文七巧》+《修辞学发凡》 | change development mode, detail, rhythm, or rhetorical relationship |
| English is grammatical but abstract or bureaucratic | Zinsser + Pinker | reveal agents and actions, remove clutter, restore reader orientation |
| English sentence lacks rhythm or emphasis | Klinkenborg + Clark | rebuild sentence units, order, endings, and length variation |
| English mechanics are inconsistent | Dreyer | perform a dedicated copyedit and house-style pass |
| Headline sounds like generic advertising | Sullivan + D&AD | return to the idea, audience tension, and image–copy division of labor |
| Chinese and English feel like translations of each other | bilingual transcreation protocol | return to a shared semantic brief and rewrite each language independently |

## Use Standard

The canon is successful only when it changes the workflow.

It is not enough to list a book in a reference section. The Copy Desk must convert the relevant principles into:

- a Copy Brief;
- candidate generation;
- language-specific drafting;
- native-language copyediting;
- read-aloud and idiomaticity tests;
- a scored Copy Quality Gate;
- regression tests using actual failed lines.
