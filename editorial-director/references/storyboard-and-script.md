# Storyboard and Script

## Purpose

Storyboard development translates sequence logic into frame-level communication intent without taking over Art Direction.

The Editorial Director owns **what the audience must encounter in each frame**. The Art Director later owns **how that frame is visually designed**.

## Storyboard Responsibility

A storyboard beat should specify, as needed:

- the editorial job;
- what the audience must understand or feel;
- what event, state, transformation, comparison, evidence, object, action, or relationship must be perceptible;
- what information must be visible in the frame;
- what must remain unresolved for the next beat;
- what visual meaning must not be lost downstream.

## Storyboard Script

Write enough scene or frame description that Art Director does not need to invent the communication logic.

Useful description dimensions include:

- subject or object required by our content;
- action or state change;
- relationship between required elements;
- before / after or cause / effect relationship;
- spatial or temporal condition when meaningful;
- evidence that must be shown rather than merely described;
- emotional or cognitive pressure that the frame must carry.

## Do Not Cross Into Art Direction

Do not specify routine design execution such as:

- exact placement percentages;
- final camera angle unless the angle itself is essential to meaning;
- font family, weight, point size, or line spacing;
- final palette;
- exact crop coordinates;
- decorative texture;
- layout geometry;
- effects used only for aesthetics.

These belong to Art Director or Production.

## When Visual Specificity Is Editorially Necessary

Some visual conditions are part of meaning and may be locked in the script.

Examples:

- the same object must appear in both physical and digital form so the transformation is unmistakable;
- a failure state must remain visible rather than be replaced by a generic success illustration;
- a before/after comparison must use equivalent evidence;
- the audience must perceive scale, absence, accumulation, isolation, or transformation for the concept to work.

Describe the semantic requirement, not the final composition.

## Reference Image Boundary

A visual reference is not a storyboard source unless its depicted content has separately been approved as usable content.

If the ECD likes a reference for mood, atmosphere, aesthetic, lighting, materiality, scale, or visual temperament:

- preserve those qualities as reference intent;
- derive our subjects, objects, scenes, and events from the Greenlit Creative Treatment;
- do not copy the reference's person, building, pose, prop, story event, or exact composition merely because it is visible there.

## Evidence-Led Frames

When screenshots, test outputs, source material, diagrams, or comparisons carry the proof, state that explicitly in the storyboard.

Do not replace evidence with decorative imagery merely to make the sequence more cinematic.

## Storyboard QA

For each beat verify:

- the frame has a necessary content function;
- it is not visually repeating another beat's semantic job;
- the described scene comes from our content, not from a style reference;
- the frame can be art-directed without rewriting the script;
- all required facts, limitations, comparisons, or evidence remain visible;
- visual intent is specific enough to preserve meaning but not so prescriptive that it becomes layout execution.
