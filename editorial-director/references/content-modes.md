# Content Modes

## Purpose

Content Mode defines the dominant communication behavior of a Social Editorial piece or an individual section.

Do not treat narrative as the default. Select the mode that best serves the Greenlit Creative Treatment.

## Core Modes

### Narrative
Use when meaning depends on change over time, lived experience, tension, or a sequence of events.

Primary question:
> What happens, changes, or is experienced?

### Explanatory
Use when the audience needs to understand a concept, mechanism, relationship, or cause.

Primary question:
> How does this work, and why?

### Demonstrative
Use when the value lies in showing a capability, transformation, output, or observable behavior.

Primary question:
> What can we actually see happening?

### Comparative
Use when meaning emerges through a relevant difference between states, methods, objects, or outcomes.

Primary question:
> What changes when A is compared with B?

### Procedural
Use when the audience needs to understand a process, workflow, or ordered action.

Primary question:
> What happens step by step?

### Persuasive / Argumentative
Use when the piece advances a judgment or interpretation that must be supported rather than merely stated.

Primary question:
> Why should the audience accept or reconsider this claim?

### Evidentiary
Use when screenshots, tests, outputs, documents, data, or observed results should carry the main editorial weight.

Primary question:
> What evidence makes this visible or credible?

### Speculative
Use for future-facing concepts, hypothetical states, conceptual worlds, or imaginative propositions.

Primary question:
> What becomes thinkable if this premise is allowed to unfold?

Speculative mode must preserve the distinction between imagination and current fact.

## Mixed Mode

Most strong pieces use a dominant mode plus one or two supporting modes.

Examples:

- Explanatory + Demonstrative
- Speculative + Narrative
- Comparative + Evidentiary
- Persuasive + Explanatory
- Procedural + Demonstrative

Do not switch modes merely for variety. A mode change should correspond to a change in editorial job.

## Mode Selection

Choose the mode by asking:

1. What does the audience need to experience cognitively?
2. What kind of proof or progression does the Treatment require?
3. Is the value in understanding, seeing, comparing, following, imagining, or being persuaded?
4. Which mode would distort the Greenlit premise if overused?

## Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

- forcing a simple product demonstration into dramatic storytelling;
- forcing a speculative concept into current-feasibility analysis;
- turning an explanation into a tutorial when no action is required;
- turning every evidence page into a comparison;
- using emotional narrative to conceal weak evidence;
- using list structure when the audience actually needs causal progression.

## Output Use

Record the dominant mode for the piece and, when useful, the mode of individual beats in the Creative Script.

Mode is a communication decision, not a visual style.
