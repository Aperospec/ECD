# Editorial Architecture

## Purpose

Editorial architecture turns a Greenlit Creative Treatment into a sequence of screen-level communication jobs before wording or visual design is finalized.

The goal is not to fill pages. The goal is to decide what the audience must understand, notice, feel, compare, verify, or reconsider at each beat.

## Start From the Greenlit Promise

Extract from the Creative Treatment:

- the project premise;
- the selected angle;
- the core proposition;
- the intended audience takeaway;
- factual / speculative / claim boundaries;
- what the piece must not imply.

These form the editorial boundary. Do not invent a new premise during scripting.

## Editorial Job

Assign one primary job to each beat. Typical jobs include:

- **Hook** — create legitimate interest in the Greenlit premise;
- **Orient** — establish what this is or what changed;
- **Define** — make a concept precise;
- **Explain** — show mechanism, cause, or structure;
- **Demonstrate** — make an ability or change perceptible;
- **Prove** — present evidence, result, comparison, or observed output;
- **Apply** — connect the idea to lived use or consequence;
- **Contrast** — expose a meaningful difference;
- **Limit** — make uncertainty, failure, or boundary visible;
- **Reframe** — change how the audience understands the subject;
- **Reflect** — create deliberate cognitive or emotional space;
- **Payoff** — fulfill the opening promise;
- **Close** — leave a judgment, implication, question, or action.

One beat may contain supporting information, but it should not perform several competing primary jobs.

## Information Priority

For each beat determine:

1. What must be understood immediately?
2. What is necessary to support that understanding?
3. What can be omitted without damaging the sequence?
4. What belongs elsewhere rather than on this beat?

Do not preserve information merely because it exists in the source material.

## Screen-Level Independence

Social Editorial is read under interruption. A beat should make sense when encountered with imperfect memory of the previous screen.

This does not mean every screen repeats context. It means the screen has a legible local purpose and does not rely on hidden explanatory debt.

## Density

Density follows the editorial job:

- high-impact / hook / payoff beats normally carry less language;
- explanation and evidence beats may carry more information;
- deliberate pauses may carry almost none;
- do not make several dense beats consecutive unless the subject genuinely requires it.

## Architecture Test

Before writing final copy, verify:

- every beat has one primary editorial job;
- no two adjacent beats perform the same job with different wording;
- every necessary concept appears before dependent concepts;
- the opening promise has a visible payoff location;
- limitations appear where they affect interpretation, not as an afterthought;
- removing any beat either improves the sequence or exposes why that beat is necessary.

If the architecture is weak, do not solve it with better copy.
