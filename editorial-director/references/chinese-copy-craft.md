# Chinese Copy Craft

## Purpose

This protocol defines the minimum standard for Simplified or Traditional Chinese audience-facing copy.

The objective is Chinese that is semantically exact, grammatically complete, idiomatic, concrete, rhythmically controlled, and appropriate to the intended voice.

## Locale and mechanics

Establish:

- Simplified or Traditional Chinese;
- target region and house style;
- quotation, punctuation, number, date, and mixed-language conventions;
- degree of formality and spokenness.

For Simplified Chinese punctuation, use GB/T 15834—2011 as the default baseline unless a publication style overrides it.

## Sentence integrity

Check whether every line expresses a complete and stable relation.

Review:

- conditions and their main clauses;
- comparisons and their terms;
- verbs and usable objects;
- definitions and judgments;
- modifier scope;
- omitted subjects;
- punctuation in relation to syntax.

An intentional fragment must be clearly supported by the page system. Punctuation cannot substitute for missing grammar.

## Semantic compatibility

For definitions, metaphors, and judgments, confirm that the related terms can reasonably be connected in the intended context.

Check:

- category compatibility;
- logical relation;
- degree of abstraction;
- whether a complement feels semantically unfinished;
- whether a repair introduces a conflicting metaphor.

## Natural collocation

Review combinations of:

- verb and object;
- adjective and noun;
- measure word and noun;
- adverb and predicate;
- connective and clause;
- temporal phrase and action;
- result or directional complement.

A grammatically possible phrase still fails when normal modern Chinese would not choose that combination.

## Subject and reference

Chinese permits omission, but every omitted subject and pronoun must remain recoverable without rereading.

Check all demonstratives, pronouns, temporal references, and elliptical links in the actual viewing context. Restore the noun when a card or headline lacks enough surrounding context.

## Word order and information focus

Place the page's new information where the reader can find it quickly. Limit preambles, nested modifiers, and delayed predicates unless the rhythm has a deliberate function.

Use topic-comment structure only when the topic is stable and the comment carries a clear new judgment.

## Internal language and public language

Editorial Jobs, analytical labels, state terms, and workflow vocabulary remain backstage. Audience copy expresses the underlying human action, image, tension, evidence, or judgment.

Before publishing an internal proposition, rewrite it for the target reader and surface.

## Concreteness

Prefer project-specific people, actions, places, sensory conditions, objects in use, decisions, and visible consequences when they express the idea more clearly than abstract nouns.

Abstract language remains appropriate when it names a precise concept. Several abstractions should not replace the main action.

## Voice and register

Maintain a stable relationship with the reader:

- narrative person;
- social distance;
- emotional temperature;
- degree of certainty;
- degree of poetic compression;
- level of explanation.

Natural Chinese is not automatically casual, and literary language is not automatically precise.

## Metaphor discipline

Identify the governing conceptual vocabulary and keep its logic stable. Related metaphors may coexist when their relationship is deliberate and understandable.

Check whether a new noun or verb changes the conceptual system rather than merely improving a sentence.

## Title, Core Line, and support

Assign distinct functions:

- **Title** — opens the image, idea, tension, or judgment;
- **Core Line** — completes the page's primary meaning;
- **Supporting Copy** — supplies only the context required for interpretation.

A title need not explain the whole page. Repetition among the three layers signals weak division of labor.

## Rhythm and read-aloud

Read high-impact lines at natural speed. Check:

- grammatical and rhetorical pauses;
- stress placement;
- accumulation of function words;
- repeated sentence endings;
- breath length;
- one-hearing comprehension;
- whether the final phrase carries the intended weight.

## Cross-page language audit

Review:

- repeated openings and syntactic frames;
- repeated contrast or parallel structures;
- overused abstract vocabulary;
- forced symmetry between paired pages;
- tonal shifts;
- metaphor drift;
- page titles that merely label sections;
- several pages making the same claim in different words.

## Alignment-ready criteria

A line is Alignment-ready when:

- grammar and punctuation reveal a complete intended relation;
- word choice and collocation are natural for the locale;
- subject and reference are stable;
- the wording preserves the accepted meaning and certainty;
- the page's copy layers divide the work effectively;
- voice and metaphor remain coherent across the sequence;
- the line reads naturally aloud;
- the expression is specific enough to belong to the project.
