# Chinese Copy Craft

## Purpose

This protocol defines the minimum linguistic and stylistic standard for Simplified or Traditional Chinese audience-facing copy.

The goal is not to make every line literary. The goal is to write Chinese that is semantically exact, grammatically complete, idiomatic, concrete, rhythmically controlled, and appropriate to the intended voice.

Use together with:

- `writing-reference-canon.md`;
- `copy-desk-workflow.md`;
- `copy-quality-gate.md`.

For Simplified Chinese punctuation, use GB/T 15834—2011 as the baseline unless the target locale or publication house style requires another convention.

## 1. Sentence Closure

A line must express a complete grammatical and semantic relation unless an intentional fragment is clearly supported by the surrounding design.

Check:

- whether an opening condition has a main clause;
- whether a comparison has both sides;
- whether a transitive verb has a usable object;
- whether a pronoun has a clear antecedent;
- whether punctuation is disguising an incomplete sentence.

Failure:

> 如果，把这一生建成一座可以走进去的档案馆

The comma does not complete the conditional structure.

Possible repairs depend on intent:

> 如果一生可以被建成一座档案馆。

or

> 把这一生，建成一座可以重新走进去的档案馆。

Do not treat a syntactic accident as poetic tension.

## 2. Semantic Category Compatibility

Check whether the two sides of a definition, metaphor, or judgment can reasonably be equated.

Failure:

> 每一份档案，都是一段完整的生活。

The sentence equates a stored record with an ongoing category of existence. The reader senses that the complement is unfinished or misclassified.

Possible repairs:

> 每一份档案，都保存着一段可以重新进入的生活记忆。

> 每一份档案，都打开一段完整的生活现场。

> 一段记忆，从来不只是一件事。

Choose according to the page function. Do not merely add a noun to make the grammar look complete if that noun changes the governing metaphor. For example, “回忆录” introduces a written memoir metaphor and may conflict with a spatial archive concept.

## 3. Natural Collocation

Test whether the verb, noun, adjective, measure word, and complement naturally belong together in modern Chinese.

Failures:

> 我获得的，都有它发生的那一刻。

Problems:

- “获得” and “发生” do not form a natural relation here;
- “它” has an unstable antecedent;
- the line imitates symmetry instead of speaking naturally.

Rewrite from meaning, not from the original syntax:

> 每一种获得，都曾在生活里真正发生过。

or, more concrete:

> 有些东西，是从某一天起，才真正成为我生活的一部分。

The best version depends on the actual scene.

## 4. Clear Subject and Reference

Chinese can omit subjects, but omission must not create ambiguity.

Check every “它”“这”“那些”“其中”“当时”“后来”:

- what exactly does it refer to;
- whether the reader can identify it without rereading;
- whether two possible antecedents compete;
- whether the reference survives when the line is isolated on a screen.

A social card often has weaker surrounding context than a paragraph. Restore the noun when needed.

## 5. Word Order and Information Focus

Place the element carrying the page's new meaning where the reader can find it quickly.

Avoid long preambles before the actual claim.

Weak:

> 在许多那些我们已经很久没有重新想起、却又在不知不觉中影响今天生活的片段里……

Prefer:

> 有些被忘记的片段，仍在影响今天的生活。

Then add only the detail the page needs.

## 6. Internal Language Is Not Audience Copy

Do not publish planning phrases such as:

- 完成情感回收；
- 建立观众认知；
- 让普通生活获得位置；
- 形成跨时连接；
- 扩大馆藏意义；
- 打开获得的部分；
- 承载不同生活质地。

These may be valid Editorial Jobs. They are not automatically valid Chinese copy.

Convert the internal proposition into a human statement, image, action, or judgment.

Internal proposition:

> Ordinary days make up most of a life.

Audience copy:

> 那些差点被忘记的日子，后来组成了大半个人生。

## 7. Concrete Before Abstract

Prefer:

- people;
- actions;
- weather;
- places;
- objects in use;
- sensory detail;
- decisions;
- visible change;
- relational consequence.

Use abstract nouns only when they add precision.

Weak:

> 能力、勇气、关系与归属真正发生的时刻。

The sentence names four abstractions but does not show what happened.

Stronger copy should use the actual scene or action that proves one or two of those ideas.

## 8. Avoid Model-Default Chinese

The following structures are not forbidden, but their repeated or unearned use is a warning:

- 不是……而是……；
- 不只是……更是……；
- 没有……只是……；
- 以另一种方式留了下来；
- 让……被看见；
- 重新定义……；
- 从此多了一种可能；
- 一种……一份……一个……；
- 那些关于……的……；
- 在时间的长河里；
- 碎片、痕迹、回响、温度、光影、旅程、答案等抽象意象的无条件堆叠。

Use a familiar form only when it creates a real distinction and the words are specific.

Run a pattern count across the entire sequence. Three or more pages using the same contrast or parallel structure normally require revision.

## 9. Do Not Force Symmetry

Paired pages do not require mirrored grammar.

Failure:

> 我获得的，都有它发生的那一刻。

> 我失去的，也有自己的档案。

The lines are symmetrical in form but unnatural in meaning.

Write each page according to its own human truth. Visual or conceptual pairing can remain without grammatical duplication.

## 10. Governing Metaphor Discipline

Identify the primary metaphor system:

- 档案馆；
- 博物馆；
- 回忆录；
- 时间线；
- 数据库；
- 房间；
- 展厅；
- 地图。

Related metaphors may coexist only when their relationship is clear.

Do not casually mix:

- “档案” as record;
- “展品” as museum object;
- “回忆录” as written memoir;
- “上传” as product interface;
- “人生地图” as navigation;

unless the combination creates deliberate meaning.

A strong noun cannot repair a confused metaphor system.

## 11. Title, Core Line, and Support Must Divide the Work

A page title does not need to explain the whole page.

Use:

- **Title** — opens the image, judgment, or tension;
- **Core Line** — completes the primary meaning;
- **Supporting Copy** — supplies only necessary context.

Weak title:

> 那些没有“大事”发生的日子，也在这里

It describes the page but lands weakly.

Possible title/core division:

> 那些差点被忘记的日子

> 后来才发现，它们组成了大半个人生。

Do not solve every weak title by making it longer.

## 12. Rhythm and Read-Aloud Test

Read every high-impact line aloud once at normal speed.

Check:

- whether the stress falls on the intended word;
- whether the pause is grammatical;
- whether “的”“了”“着”“过”“就是”“其实” accumulate;
- whether the line can be understood after one hearing;
- whether it sounds like modern Chinese rather than translated strategy language;
- whether the sentence ending carries weight.

Short does not automatically mean rhythmic. Long does not automatically mean sophisticated.

## 13. Punctuation

Punctuation must reflect syntax and meaning.

Do not use:

- a comma after “如果” to create artificial drama;
- excessive em dashes or ellipses as substitutes for sentence structure;
- quotation marks around ordinary words merely to create distance;
- colon-heavy writing that feels like a slide deck;
- English punctuation in Chinese text without a deliberate mixed-language convention.

For Simplified Chinese, follow GB/T 15834—2011 for baseline mechanics.

For Traditional Chinese, follow the selected locale's house style consistently, including quotation marks and punctuation placement.

## 14. Freshness Test

Ask:

- could this sentence appear unchanged in a travel post, graduation post, AI product post, or sentimental advertisement;
- does it rely on generic emotional nouns rather than this project's specific mechanism;
- does the sentence reveal something only this page can say;
- is the line memorable because of thought, or merely because of decoration.

If it can belong anywhere, it probably belongs nowhere.

## 15. Required Chinese Copy Edit

For every visible line, record internally:

```markdown

```

Do not expose this worksheet by default.

## Chinese Hard Failures

A line cannot enter an Alignment-ready Script when it contains:

- an unintentionally incomplete sentence;
- obvious collocation or category mismatch;
- an unclear pronoun affecting meaning;
- punctuation used to hide malformed syntax;
- internal planning language presented as audience copy;
- a metaphor collision that changes the concept;
- forced symmetry that produces unnatural Chinese;
- a generic AI-style phrase carrying the page's main meaning;
- a title that the ECD must personally complete or rewrite before it makes sense.
