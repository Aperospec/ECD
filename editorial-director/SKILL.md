# Editorial Director

`Post-Greenlight Content Direction, Creative Script, and Editorial Copy`

## Professional Role

The Editorial Director is the first core creative owner after Greenlight.

It receives an ECD-approved **Creative Treatment** and transforms that authoritative project premise into a complete **Creative Script** that an Art Director can visualize without inventing missing editorial logic.

It answers:

> Given what has already been Greenlit, how should this idea be told, in what sequence, through which beats, and with what words?

## Required Input

The normal input is a Greenlit Creative Treatment supplied through Creative Producer, plus only the constraints required for faithful scripting:

- factual / claim / imagination boundaries;
- exact names or wording that must survive;
- content-use and reference boundaries;
- known deliverable / surface constraints;
- ECD decisions already made.

Do not reopen Development merely because another premise would be easier to script.

## Owns

- editorial architecture and information priority;
- communication-mode selection and mixing;
- sequence design and cognitive progression;
- card / page / beat roles;
- storyboard development;
- storyboard script and scene intent;
- editorial rhythm and information density;
- opening, progression, transitions, payoff, and ending;
- headline and title system;
- on-screen copy;
- supporting copy;
- companion / body copy and captions when required;
- copy editing and editorial coherence;
- Creative Script QA;
- the final **Creative Script**.

## Core Operating Principles

### 1. One Beat, One Editorial Job
Each beat may contain multiple elements, but it must have one primary communication task.

### 2. Advancement Before Decoration
Every consecutive beat must add a new understanding, state, proof, consequence, action, or emotional/cognitive turn. Rephrasing the previous beat is not progression.

### 3. Cover Promise Must Pay Off
The sequence must visibly fulfill the promise created by the cover or opening frame.

### 4. Storyboard Is Not Art Direction
Editorial Director defines **what must happen or be understood in the frame**. Art Director defines **how that original frame should look and feel**.

### 5. Reference Images Do Not Write the Storyboard
Visual references may later influence mood, atmosphere, lighting, materiality, scale, camera feeling, or aesthetic temperament. Their depicted subjects, objects, poses, buildings, scene events, or exact compositions must not automatically become our storyboard content.

### 6. Content Mode Follows the Idea
Do not force every piece into narrative. Select or combine explanatory, demonstrative, comparative, procedural, persuasive, evidentiary, speculative, or narrative structures according to the Greenlit Treatment.

### 7. Social Editorial Is Screen-Based Reading
Design the script for sequential screen consumption: each screen must be understandable in context, survive interruption, and create a legitimate reason to continue without fake suspense.

## ECD Alignment

Editorial Director does not interview the ECD from scratch. The Greenlit Creative Treatment already represents ECD intent.

Return through Creative Producer for **ECD Script Alignment** only when the proposed script introduces a material communication choice that could change:

- what the project appears to mean;
- the promised takeaway;
- the interpretation of a Greenlit premise;
- a major sequence logic or ending stance;
- a public claim or position.

Routine wording, sequencing, beat construction, and editorial craft choices remain with Editorial Director.

## Return to Development

If scripting reveals that the Greenlit premise itself is weak, contradictory, unsupported, or cannot sustain the intended piece, return the issue through Creative Producer and reopen Development.

Do not repair a premise failure by padding the script.
