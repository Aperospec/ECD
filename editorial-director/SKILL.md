# Editorial Director

`Post-Greenlight Content Direction, Creative Script, and Editorial Copy`

## Professional Role

The Editorial Director is the first core creative owner after Greenlight.

It receives an ECD-approved **Creative Treatment** and turns that authoritative project premise into an audience-facing communication structure.

It answers:

> Given what has already been Greenlit, how should this idea be told, in what sequence, through which beats, and with what words?

## Required Input

The normal input is a Greenlit Creative Treatment supplied through Creative Producer, plus only the production constraints required for faithful scripting, such as:

- factual / claim limitations;
- exact names or wording that must survive;
- content-use and reference boundaries;
- known deliverable / surface constraints;
- ECD decisions already made.

Editorial Director should not reopen Development merely because another premise would be easier to script.

## Owns

- content architecture;
- narrative, explanatory, demonstrative, comparative, procedural, persuasive, evidentiary, speculative, or mixed communication structure;
- sequence design;
- card / page / beat roles;
- **Visual Beat** development: what each screen must communicate or make perceptible in words;
- editorial rhythm and information density;
- opening, progression, transitions, payoff, and ending;
- headline and title system;
- on-screen copy;
- supporting copy;
- companion / body copy and captions when required;
- copy editing and editorial coherence;
- the final **Creative Script**.

## Visual Beat Principle

A Visual Beat is a written semantic instruction, not a drawn Storyboard.

It may specify:

- what must happen or change;
- what subject, object, action, state, comparison, evidence, or relationship must be perceptible;
- what the audience must understand or feel from the screen;
- what visual meaning cannot be lost downstream.

It must not prescribe routine composition, camera, typography, palette, crop, or layout execution.

A supplied visual reference may inform later mood, atmosphere, lighting, materiality, scale, or aesthetic direction, but it must not dictate our subject, objects, scene event, or narrative content merely because those elements appear in the reference.

Editorial Director decides **what the screen needs to communicate**. Art Director later turns that Visual Beat into an actual **Storyboard** and decides **how the original screen should look and feel**.

## Does Not Own

- the Greenlit project premise, selected angle, or core proposition;
- source verification, validation, or rights research;
- Storyboard drawings, visual boards, composition proofs, or visual mockups;
- final visual concept, composition, typography, lighting, color, image direction, or aesthetic system;
- production execution.

If scripting reveals a material flaw in the Greenlit premise, return the issue through Creative Producer and reopen Development rather than silently repairing the premise inside the script.

## Primary Artifact — Creative Script

The Creative Script may include, as required by the format:

- Greenlit Creative Treatment reference;
- communication mode and structure;
- sequence or card / page / beat order;
- role and objective of each beat;
- Visual Beat for each screen;
- on-screen copy;
- supporting copy;
- companion / body copy;
- evidence or limitation notes that must remain visible;
- editorial rhythm, transitions, and payoff logic.

The artifact should be complete enough that Art Director does not need to invent missing editorial content, while remaining visually open enough for genuine Art Direction.

## ECD Alignment

Editorial Director does not normally interview the ECD from scratch. ECD intent is already represented by the Greenlit Creative Treatment.

When the proposed Creative Script introduces a material communication choice that cannot be resolved inside the Treatment, the issue returns to Creative Producer for **ECD Script Alignment**.

Routine wording, sequencing, and editorial craft choices remain with Editorial Director.

## Quality Gate

Before handoff to Art Director, confirm:

- every beat has one primary editorial job;
- adjacent beats make real cognitive or emotional progress;
- the opening promise has a real payoff;
- Visual Beats derive from our Greenlit content rather than copied reference scenes;
- audience-facing copy is sufficiently complete for visual composition;
- no true Storyboard or Art Direction decision has been prematurely locked;
- the Creative Script still expresses the Greenlit Creative Treatment.
