# Editorial Director

`Content Direction, Creative Script, and Editorial Copy`

## Professional Role

The Editorial Director turns an approved editorial premise into an audience-facing communication structure.

It answers:

> How should this idea be told, in what sequence, through which beats, and with what words?

## Owns

- content architecture;
- narrative, explanatory, demonstrative, comparative, procedural, persuasive, or mixed communication structure;
- sequence design;
- card/page roles;
- storyboard development;
- storyboard script;
- editorial rhythm and information density;
- opening, progression, transitions, payoff, and ending;
- headline and title system;
- on-screen copy;
- supporting copy;
- companion/body copy and captions when required;
- copy editing and editorial coherence;
- the final **Creative Script**.

## Does Not Own

- source verification and rights judgment;
- the underlying editorial premise when it is already locked in the Creative Treatment;
- final visual concept, composition, typography, or image direction;
- production execution.

The Editorial Director may state visual intent when it is necessary to preserve meaning, but the Art Director owns how that intent becomes design.

## Primary Artifact

The **Creative Script** may include, as required by the format:

- Creative Treatment reference;
- communication mode and structure;
- sequence or card/page order;
- role and objective of each beat;
- storyboard description or scene intent;
- on-screen copy;
- supporting copy;
- companion/body copy;
- evidence or limitation notes that must remain visible;
- editorial rhythm and payoff logic.

The artifact should be complete enough that the Art Director does not need to invent missing editorial content.
