# Social Editorial Design System

## Purpose

This document defines the design-only method used after an ECD-aligned Creative Script and selected visual route exist.

It translates editorial meaning and exact copy into a mobile-native visual system. It does not determine premise, sequence, or copy.

Apply `copy-aware-composition.md` whenever a screen combines required copy with imagery.

## Viewing conditions

Establish:

- aspect ratio and pixel dimensions;
- feed preview versus full-screen behavior;
- swipe, scroll, or static use;
- UI overlays and safe areas;
- approximate physical viewing width;
- evidence-inspection requirements;
- companion-copy relationship.

## Perceptual hierarchy

Design intentional perceptual passes:

1. first glance — dominant subject, gesture, fact, title, or visual relationship;
2. second glance — decisive explanation or supporting relationship;
3. third glance — support, evidence, attribution, or limitation.

Not every screen needs three layers, but the priority remains explicit.

## Dominant visual action

Each screen has one primary visual action that carries its page function. Secondary elements support rather than compete.

The dominant action may be image-led, type-led, evidence-led, diagram-led, spatial, comparative, or intentionally quiet.

## Scale and salience

Use meaningful differences in scale, contrast, position, depth, and density. Evaluate hierarchy in representative proofs rather than treating numeric ratios as fixed rules.

## Typography as visual structure

Typography may establish geometry, rhythm, emphasis, depth, or interaction with imagery when that relationship supports the Script.

Control:

- recognition under crop or overlap;
- semantic line breaks;
- scale hierarchy;
- positive and negative space;
- depth order;
- consistency across variants.

Exact audience copy is not an afterthought. Its actual length and hierarchy must influence composition before Production-intent imagery is generated or selected.

## Copy-Aware Composition

Before committing an image intended for a final screen:

1. place the exact Script copy into target geometry with a defensible provisional font metric;
2. determine hierarchy and provisional line breaks;
3. establish the copy footprint and minimum readable scale;
4. identify protected subject, face, product, action, interface, and evidence zones;
5. choose the primary image–type relationship;
6. define quiet zones, overlap, occlusion, local contrast, crop latitude, and layer separation;
7. translate those decisions into image-generation, sourcing, editing, and masking constraints.

Record the result in a Copy-Aware Layout Map.

Valid primary relationships include:

- separated / quiet-zone;
- contained in negative space;
- controlled overlap;
- subject–type occlusion;
- silhouette wrap;
- typographic frame;
- type-led composition;
- evidence-led composition.

Do not reduce this method to `leave space on one side`. Avoidance is one option; deliberate interlock may be the correct one.

## Exploratory and Production-intent imagery

Exploratory Image-World Studies may test atmosphere, material, light, or visual language before copy geometry is resolved.

They must remain explicitly exploratory. They do not become final page images merely because they are visually successful.

Any image reasonably expected to enter a final screen is Production-intent and requires a Copy-Aware Layout Map before generation, selection, crop, or commitment.

## Depth and physical presence

Use occlusion, scale, perspective, focus, contact, value, and atmosphere to establish intentional planes.

When a subject is meant to feel dimensional, preserve believable volume, perspective, grounding, material response, and edge behavior.

Copy and subjects may occupy different depth planes, but overlap must preserve required letter anatomy and essential visual information.

## Grid and negative space

Build an underlying grid appropriate to the surface and content. Break it only when the violation strengthens meaning or energy.

Treat empty space as active structure for focus, pace, hierarchy, evidence isolation, copy footprint, and readability.

Negative space is planned from actual copy rather than assumed from a generic placeholder.

## Text readability

Design text and image together. Establish quiet zones, local contrast, line length, scale, and depth relationships before relying on auxiliary containers.

Any container, label, tag, or module should belong to the concept and information system rather than act as an unplanned repair.

Do not rescue a copy-blind image by shrinking required text below a defensible mobile threshold or placing every text block on an emergency opaque rectangle.

## Cover and body grammar

The cover establishes the opening promise and visual identity at feed speed.

Body pages preserve the shared grammar while varying image / type emphasis, density, scale, evidence, and spatial behavior according to each beat.

The ending releases, redirects, or resolves the visual energy according to the Script payoff.

## Page archetypes

Choose according to the accepted Written Visual Requirements and Frame Script:

- hero statement;
- question or tension;
- scene or transformation;
- annotated evidence;
- process or flow;
- comparison;
- dominant fact;
- principle or quotation;
- pause;
- payoff or conclusion.

Archetypes guide design behavior, not fixed templates.

## Evidence treatment

When evidence carries the claim:

- preserve authenticity and relevant context;
- make critical content inspectable;
- locate annotations without obscuring proof;
- keep limitations near the evidence they qualify;
- distinguish evidence from reconstructed or illustrative material;
- reserve evidence protection zones before placing copy or generating surrounding imagery.

## Sequence rhythm

Create continuity through accepted typography, grid, color relationships, image treatment, copy behavior, and recurring identifiers.

Create progression through purposeful changes in:

- density;
- value;
- color;
- scale;
- depth;
- viewpoint;
- image / type balance;
- evidence intensity;
- movement and pause.

## Type-Fit Proof

After representative imagery exists, compose exact copy deterministically at target geometry.

Verify:

- wording, punctuation, and hierarchy;
- intended line behavior;
- copy footprint and local contrast;
- protected visual content;
- image–type depth and masking;
- actual-width readability;
- safe areas and representative variants.

A failed fit returns to the earliest failed object: copy, hierarchy, Layout Map, image asset, typography implementation, or Deliverable Contract.

## Mobile proof

Run:

- feed-preview recognition;
- blur / squint hierarchy;
- actual-width readability;
- UI-overlay and safe-area review;
- exact-copy and line-break verification;
- Layout Map fidelity;
- absence of emergency fit repair.

## Visual quality gate

Evaluate:

- first-glance meaning;
- hierarchy and reading path;
- mobile readability;
- image–type integration;
- fidelity to the Copy-Aware Layout Map;
- composition and negative space;
- subject presence and depth;
- evidence integrity;
- sequence coherence and differentiation;
- originality and fidelity to the selected visual route.

A design proceeds when representative proof clearly expresses the accepted visual premise under target viewing conditions, exact copy fits without hidden redesign, and Creative Producer can verify that Production-intent imagery was developed from copy-aware constraints.
