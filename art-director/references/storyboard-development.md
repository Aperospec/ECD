# Storyboard Development

## Purpose

Storyboard is the Art Director's first visual translation of an approved Creative Script.

It converts written **Visual Beats** into low-cost visual panels so the studio can evaluate visual interpretation before final production.

Storyboard is not final art and not a replacement for the Creative Script.

## Input Fidelity

Every Storyboard panel must map to an approved Visual Beat and preserve:

- the beat's editorial job;
- required subject / object / action / state / relationship;
- evidence or limitation that must remain visible;
- exact editorial meaning;
- locked copy or names when shown;
- reference-use boundaries.

The Art Director may invent visual solutions, but not new editorial content.

## Storyboard Forms

Choose the lowest-cost format that makes the material visual decision legible:

- rough sketch;
- composition thumbnail;
- grayscale wireframe;
- low-fidelity generated visual;
- collage or mood / composition board;
- simple scene mockup;
- type–image relationship proof.

Do not spend final-production effort on a direction that has not yet earned approval.

## What the Storyboard Must Test

As relevant, test:

- subject and environment relationship;
- scale and depth;
- visual center and reading path;
- image–type relationship;
- visual differentiation between adjacent screens;
- mood and atmosphere;
- visual metaphor or perceptual transformation;
- whether evidence is still visible and credible;
- whether the visual survives the intended viewing condition;
- whether the sequence feels coherent without collapsing into one repeated template.

## Reference DNA Without Copying

Treat supplied references as attribute sources, not scene templates.

Potentially reusable qualities include:

- lighting behavior;
- color relationships;
- materiality;
- spatial scale;
- depth;
- atmospheric density;
- emotional temperature;
- image grain or surface behavior;
- composition temperament;
- typography compatibility.

Unless separately authorized as content, do not transfer:

- subject identity;
- person / character;
- building;
- product / object / prop;
- pose;
- narrative event;
- exact camera / crop;
- exact composition;
- logo / symbol;
- distinctive branded treatment.

The Storyboard must be derived from **our Creative Script**, then informed by approved reference qualities.

## ECD Visual Alignment

Use Storyboard for ECD alignment when visual interpretation is materially subjective or direction-defining.

The Art Director should provide:

- the recommended direction;
- enough representative panels to make the direction perceptible;
- a short explanation of mood / atmosphere / aesthetic intent;
- the main tradeoff or risk;
- any material alternative only when it would genuinely change the work.

Creative Producer translates this into ECD-ready alignment.

Do not ask the ECD to decide routine production geometry.

## Storyboard QA

Before proceeding to full Art Direction, confirm:

- every panel maps to the intended Visual Beat;
- no reference scene has been lightly redrawn or re-skinned;
- adjacent screens have purposeful visual differentiation;
- mood and visual world support the Greenlit premise;
- evidence is not replaced by decoration;
- the direction can survive the target surface;
- unresolved problems are correctly routed to Editorial Director, Development, or Research rather than hidden visually.
