# Visual Reference Canon

## Purpose

This canon supplies method sources for Art Director's visual-development practice.

Books, academic methods, and professional processes are treated as compressed craft systems. Extract generalized principles; do not reproduce source text, copy signature examples, or imitate a named artist's style.

## Priority order

When methods conflict, prioritize:

1. fidelity to the Greenlit Treatment and Accepted Script;
2. ECD intent and recorded authority;
3. legible visual meaning at the target surface;
4. originality, rights, and reference boundaries;
5. formal quality and sequence coherence;
6. production feasibility.

## Design framing

### Kees Dorst — frame creation and frame innovation

Operational contribution:

- distinguish the user's underlying objective from an initial solution form;
- identify values, themes, and relationships that define the design problem;
- generate alternative frames before optimizing one solution;
- detect goal reformulation that solves a different problem.

Use for Visual Problem Statements and Concept Route development.

## Visual meaning and methodology

### Gunther Kress and Theo van Leeuwen — visual grammar

Operational contribution:

- analyze representation, viewer relationship, composition, salience, framing, and information value;
- treat images as organized meaning rather than object lists.

Use for reference reading, hierarchy, and viewer-position analysis.

### Gillian Rose — visual methodologies

Operational contribution:

- examine the image, its production, its circulation, and its audience context;
- distinguish formal attraction from contextual meaning;
- account for medium and viewing conditions.

Use for reference-role assignment and contextual analysis.

## Visual structure

### Bruce Block — visual structure

Operational contribution:

- treat space, line, shape, value, color, movement, and rhythm as controllable variables;
- create visual progression across a sequence;
- separate surface continuity from structural continuity.

Use for formal studies, Storyboard, and color / sequence systems.

### Marcos Mateu-Mestre — composition and visual narrative

Operational contribution:

- organize large masses, contrast, movement, and focal pressure;
- use composition to direct narrative perception;
- solve the frame before surface finish.

Use for thumbnails, value studies, and Anchor Keyframes.

## Concept and world development

### Professional concept-design process literature

Operational contribution:

- research before resolution;
- explore materially different directions;
- move from rough studies to representative keyframes;
- coordinate environment, object, character, and world logic;
- show process evidence rather than only a polished result.

Use for Concept Routes, World-Logic Boards, and anchors.

### Scott Robertson and related spatial-construction methods

Operational contribution:

- build believable form and space through perspective, proportion, and basic geometry;
- use construction knowledge to support invented environments and objects.

Use for spatial studies and production feasibility.

## Color and light

### James Gurney and related color / light practice

Operational contribution:

- relate light sources, material response, environment color, atmosphere, and depth;
- connect color design to physical plausibility and emotional purpose.

Use for Style Boards, Anchor Keyframes, and color scripts.

## Collaborative visual inquiry

### Visual Thinking Strategies

Operational contribution:

- begin from visible evidence;
- paraphrase accurately;
- connect observations without replacing the viewer's judgment;
- use questions to build shared interpretation.

Use for Visual Intent Elicitation and ECD-friendly alignment.

### Repertory-grid and triadic-comparison methods

Operational contribution:

- derive the user's own evaluation dimensions by comparing alternatives;
- avoid forcing a prewritten vocabulary of style labels.

Use when preference is clear but difficult to articulate.

### Attribute–perception–value laddering

Operational contribution:

- connect visible properties to perceived effects and project value;
- translate broad aesthetic judgments into actionable criteria.

Use for intent elicitation and critique.

## Art criticism and formal analysis

Operational contribution:

- separate description, analysis, interpretation, and evaluation;
- identify why a visual result succeeds or fails;
- route rework to the earliest failed object.

Use for reference reading and visual failure diagnosis.

## Canon-to-workflow map

| Visual question | Primary method layer |
|---|---|
| What problem are we actually solving? | design framing |
| What does the ECD value in the material? | evidence-based inquiry, comparison, laddering |
| Why does a reference work? | visual grammar, formal analysis, visual methodologies |
| How should a metaphor become a system? | relational mapping and concept design |
| Which route should govern the work? | concept development and comparative evaluation |
| Does the frame work before finish? | composition, value, spatial, and shape studies |
| Does the sequence progress? | visual structure and color-script methods |
| Why did a result fail? | art criticism and first-failed-object diagnosis |

## Use standard

A method source is useful only when it changes the workflow, proof, or evaluation. Source names remain background references; project execution uses the operational protocols in this directory.
