# Visual Critique and Failure Diagnosis

## Purpose

This protocol turns visual approval, discomfort, rejection, or “ugly” feedback into a professional diagnosis and the smallest valid rework route.

Do not assume that a bad final-looking image is a Production Artist problem. Locate the earliest visual object where the failure became true.

## Critique Sequence

Use:

```text
Description
→ Formal Analysis
→ Interpretation
→ Evaluation against brief
→ First-failed-object diagnosis
→ Rework recommendation
```

Separate what is visible from why it fails.

## Evaluation Dimensions

### 1. Brief Fidelity

- Does the work solve the Visual Problem Statement?
- Is it still the project the ECD aligned?
- Has ease of generation replaced the accepted goal?

### 2. Metaphor Fidelity

- Are useful relationships transferred?
- Or are source-domain objects used as decorative skin?
- Does the metaphor generate the world, or merely label it?

### 3. Reference Fidelity and Distance

- Did the work use only authorized reference roles?
- Did it preserve the valuable structure?
- Is it too close to the source scene or too distant from what the ECD valued?

### 4. World / Scene Coherence

- Do all frames belong to one world?
- Are regions, routes, materials, scale, and logic consistent?
- Has a world-led project collapsed into isolated scenes?

### 5. Formal Quality

- dominant silhouette;
- composition and center of gravity;
- value grouping;
- scale and depth;
- salience and reading path;
- line, movement, and rhythm;
- color and light logic;
- negative space;
- relation to copy.

### 6. Narrative Salience

- What does the viewer perceive first?
- Is that the intended idea?
- Does emotion, character, scenery, typography, or spectacle steal priority from the real subject?

### 7. Affective Fit

- Does the emotional temperature fit the Treatment and Script?
- Has the image fallen into generic sentiment, cinematic melancholy, sterile futurism, luxury branding, tourism advertising, or game-key-art spectacle?

### 8. Sequence Differentiation

- Do pages progress formally and semantically?
- Are composition, scale, value, and color varied for a reason?
- Are repeated backs, rainy reflections, sunsets, or blue–orange grading substituting for visual development?

### 9. Production Quality

- anatomy, geometry, perspective, contact, material, and lighting;
- masking and edge quality;
- text accuracy and layout;
- crop, resolution, consistency, and export.

Only this final category belongs automatically to Production Artist.

## First-Failed-Object Routing

| Failure first appears in | Owner / object to reopen |
|---|---|
| accepted project meaning was visually reformulated | Visual Problem Framing / Art Director |
| ECD intuition was misunderstood or never elicited | Visual Intent Elicitation / Creative Producer + Art Director |
| reference role or transferable qualities were wrong | Reference Reading and Transfer / Art Director |
| metaphor became literal decoration or changed the project | Visual Metaphor Map / Art Director |
| visual system, world logic, or viewer relationship was wrong | Concept Route / Art Director |
| large composition, value, scale, or topology was wrong | Formal Studies / Art Director |
| page staging or sequence repetition was wrong | Storyboard / Art Director |
| representative image language failed | Anchor Keyframe / Art Director |
| hierarchy, typography, or page design failed | Representative Design Comp / Art Director |
| accepted visual direction was executed incorrectly | Production Artist |
| Script meaning or copy caused the visual failure | Editorial Director / Copy Desk |

## “Ugly” Feedback Protocol

When the ECD says the result is ugly, weak, wrong, cheap, or not the intended feeling:

1. stop broad generation;
2. do not defend the prompt or explain the intended meaning as if intention cures the image;
3. compare the output with the Visual Problem Statement, selected route, anchors, and reference-transfer record;
4. identify the dominant failure categories;
5. state the diagnosis in plain language;
6. distinguish professional diagnosis from any remaining subjective question;
7. reopen the first failed object;
8. preserve unaffected decisions;
9. create a new low-cost proof before another batch.

## Example Diagnosis — Theme-Park Montage Failure

Observed:

- repeated mountain park background;
- blue–orange evening grade;
- character-centered rain and family vignettes;
- recognizable rides and cabins dominate;
- frames feel like tourism or film stills;
- the world logic is not legible across pages.

Diagnosis:

- **Visual Problem failure:** “explorable life-world” became “emotional scenes in a park.”
- **Reference-transfer failure:** source objects were copied while navigability and world structure were underdeveloped.
- **Metaphor failure:** amusement park became a literal setting rather than a relational model for life.
- **Concept-route failure:** world-led concept collapsed into character-led cinematic vignettes.
- **Sequence failure:** repeated grade and staging create cosmetic consistency without progression.

Correct route:

- reopen Visual Problem, Reference Reading, Metaphor Map, and Concept Route;
- do not ask Production Artist to simply rerender six images.

## No Blind Variant Rule

After rejection, do not:

- produce six more images with a revised prompt;
- change only palette, weather, or camera;
- generate many variants and outsource diagnosis to the ECD;
- preserve a broken visual system because some images are expensive or detailed;
- call a concept failure a “style preference” without evidence;
- ask the ECD to rewrite the visual brief from scratch.

## Quality Gate

A visual direction may proceed only when:

- the critique addresses the actual project and accepted artifacts;
- the earliest failure is identified;
- rework is scoped to that object and affected downstream work;
- the ECD can understand the diagnosis without specialist vocabulary;
- the next proof is cheaper and more diagnostic than another full batch.
