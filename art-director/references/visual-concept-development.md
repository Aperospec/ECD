# Visual Concept Development

## Purpose

Visual Concept Development converts an accepted Visual Problem Statement, Visual Intent Record, Reference Reading, and Visual Metaphor Map into an original, testable visual direction before Storyboard and high-fidelity production.

The goal is not to decorate the Script. It is to discover the visual system capable of carrying the Script across the whole sequence.

## Required Inputs

- Visual Problem Statement;
- Activated Visual inputs and authority classes;
- original references plus Reference Reading Records;
- Visual Metaphor Map when applicable;
- Accepted Creative Script and Written Visual Beats;
- target aspect ratio, platform, and viewing condition;
- existing visual decisions and locks.

## Separate the Board Functions

Do not create one undifferentiated mood board.

### Mood Board

Answers:

- What should the audience feel on entering this world?
- Is the relationship inviting, distant, private, kinetic, solemn, playful, or unsettling?
- What emotional temperature must survive?

Use abstraction, atmosphere, material fragments, and selective imagery. Do not let a single complete reference scene dictate the solution.

### Style Board

Answers:

- What formal language will carry the idea?
- What is the range of realism, edge, texture, material, detail density, color behavior, and light behavior?
- What visual qualities are compatible or incompatible?

Style does not define content or world logic by itself.

### Concept Board

Answers:

- What is the governing visual idea?
- How does the core metaphor become perceptible?
- What is the subject–environment relationship?
- How does the project differ from familiar genre defaults?

### World Logic Board

Required for coherent multi-frame worlds.

Answers:

- What regions or systems exist?
- How do people enter, move, pause, return, or leave?
- How are time, memory, evidence, loss, or unfinished material spatialized?
- What can never appear because it would break the concept?
- How do cover, body pages, and payoff remain recognizably one world?

### Content / Asset Board

Use only for authorized subjects, objects, architecture, products, interfaces, or evidence that must actually appear.

Do not use content boards to smuggle reference scenes into original work.

## Concept Route Requirement

For a new or materially unresolved visual system, develop **two or three materially different routes**.

Routes must differ in governing structure, viewer relationship, or world logic—not merely in palette, weather, crop, or rendering style.

Each route should include:

```markdown
# Concept Route [A / B / C]

Route name:
Visual thesis:
What the audience perceives first:
World / scene logic:
Subject–environment hierarchy:
How the metaphor works:
Reference qualities used:
Reference qualities rejected:
Formal structure:
Mood and emotional distance:
Sequence potential:
Principal strength:
Principal risk:
What this route could become if mishandled:
Low-cost proof:
Recommendation status:
```

## Example Route Differences

For a life archive / life amusement-world project:

### Route A — Unified Life Mountain

- one monumental world;
- regions embedded in one central structure;
- strong establishing image and spatial continuity;
- risk: commercial theme-park poster.

### Route B — Memory Infrastructure

- bridges, loops, thresholds, lifts, platforms, and linked zones rather than recognizable rides;
- more abstract and architectural;
- risk: cold institutional distance.

### Route C — Living Memory Landscape

- life scenes grow as active regions inside a changing landscape;
- strongest emotional intimacy;
- risk: collapsing into isolated cinematic vignettes.

These are routes because they change the visual system, not because they use different color grades.

## ECD Route Alignment

Creative Producer presents:

- the recommended route;
- one clear visual thesis per route;
- low-cost visual evidence;
- the principal tradeoff;
- plain-language comparison axes;
- the route that best preserves the Treatment and Script.

The ECD may answer in natural language, for example:

> A feels closest. B is too cold. C looks like a movie still.

Art Director translates that into formal decisions and records the original wording.

Do not present many cosmetic variants or ask the ECD to select professional jargon.

## Formal Development After Route Selection

Once a route is selected or professionally accepted, solve the large visual relationships before finish.

### Composition thumbnails

Create enough small studies to test:

- dominant silhouette;
- center of gravity;
- path of attention;
- subject–environment scale;
- depth and overlap;
- negative space;
- title / image territory;
- continuity with adjacent frames.

Do not polish weak thumbnails.

### Value studies

Use grayscale or simplified value blocks to test:

- focal hierarchy;
- large-shape separation;
- readable depth;
- whether lighting supports the meaning;
- whether atmosphere is hiding disorder.

### Spatial / topology studies

For world-led work, test:

- region arrangement;
- route continuity;
- entrances, thresholds, loops, dead ends, overlooks, and unfinished areas;
- how establishing and close views belong to the same world;
- whether the viewer can mentally navigate the system.

### Scale studies

Test:

- human-to-world relationship;
- intimacy versus monumentality;
- whether people are protagonists, guides, evidence of use, or background scale markers;
- whether repeated close-ups unintentionally change the visual premise.

### Motion and line studies

Test:

- directional energy;
- ascent, descent, return, pause, collision, and flow;
- how curves, diagonals, verticals, and horizontals support the sequence.

## Storyboard Entry Gate

Storyboard begins only when:

- the governing route is selected;
- the visual problem and metaphor are stable enough to stage each beat;
- reference-transfer rules are clear;
- large formal relationships have plausible solutions;
- the ECD has aligned any materially subjective direction.

A Storyboard should not be used to discover a still-unframed visual concept across final-looking panels.

## Color Script

After the route and anchor direction are credible, create a compact color / light progression across the whole sequence.

A Color Script controls:

- emotional progression;
- contrast and affinity between pages;
- light-source logic;
- time, memory, evidence, or narrative state;
- pauses and climaxes;
- continuity of one world.

It is not one palette copied onto every page.

Record:

```markdown
| Beat | Emotional / editorial state | Value structure | Color relationship | Light behavior | Contrast with adjacent beat |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
```

## Low-Fidelity Tool Use

Image generation may be used for:

- rough visual hypotheses;
- route boards;
- low-cost composition exploration;
- material or light studies;
- non-authoritative concept frames.

Label these clearly as exploratory.

Do not let high rendering quality upgrade an unaligned route into an accepted direction.

## Failure Conditions

- only one route is explored when the visual system is materially unresolved;
- routes differ only in style or color;
- one reference image supplies concept, content, composition, and style without separation;
- the world logic is absent for a multi-frame world;
- character close-ups replace a world-led brief;
- thumbnails, value, and topology are skipped because image generation can produce finish quickly;
- every page repeats the same blue–orange grade or cinematic staging;
- Storyboard or batch images begin before route alignment;
- a route is selected because it is easiest to prompt rather than most faithful.

## Output State

Concept Development may be:

- `Working`;
- `Proposed for Visual Direction Alignment`;
- `ECD-Aligned`;
- `Accepted for Formal Development`;
- `Reopened`.

Only an aligned or professionally accepted route may become the basis of the Storyboard and Anchor Keyframes.
