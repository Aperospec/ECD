# ECD-Friendly Visual Alignment

## Purpose

This protocol lets a non-specialist Executive Creative Director make material visual decisions without being asked to speak like an Art Director.

The ECD may respond through preference, analogy, rejection, emotional judgment, or plain-language comparison. Art Director is responsible for converting that response into professional visual criteria and preserving the source wording.

## Alignment Objects

Use visual alignment at two distinct gates when material:

### Visual Direction Alignment

Before Storyboard / anchor production, align:

- the Visual Problem Statement;
- reference interpretation;
- visual metaphor;
- concept route;
- world / scene hierarchy;
- major audience relationship.

### Anchor / Design Alignment

Before broad production, align:

- World / Cover Anchor;
- Representative Body Anchor;
- Representative Design Comp when relevant;
- actual image language, hierarchy, and continuity;
- the decisions that will become visual locks.

Do not collapse both gates when the first direction is still conceptually uncertain.

## Art Director Responsibilities

Before asking the ECD anything:

1. inspect the Treatment, Script, visual inputs, and references;
2. form a professional hypothesis;
3. identify the evidence supporting it;
4. recommend a direction;
5. define the consequence of alternatives;
6. decide whether words or low-cost visual comparisons will produce the clearest decision.

The ECD should not receive an empty “What do you want?” prompt.

## Good Question Design

Ask about audience experience and visible consequences.

Prefer:

- Should the whole world be the first thing we see, with people proving it is lived in, or should one person's emotion dominate the frame?
- If the recognizable rides disappear, should the space still feel like a place that can be explored, climbed, revisited, and extended?
- Which failure is worse: looking like a commercial attraction poster, a cold future institution, or a sentimental movie still?
- Should all pages feel like regions of one continuous world, or like separate memories connected only by color and typography?

Avoid:

- Do you prefer isometric environmental concept art or cinematic 35 mm coverage?
- Which lens and color temperature should be used?
- What is your preferred spatial topology?
- Please write the production prompt you want.

Technical decisions remain with Art Director after the ECD clarifies the intended experience.

## Recommendation Rule

Do not present equal options without judgment.

For each material alternative, state:

- why it serves the project;
- what it sacrifices;
- what it may accidentally become;
- which option Art Director recommends and why.

The ECD may reject the recommendation, but should not have to perform the analysis first.

## Visual Evidence Rule

Use text when the distinction is conceptual and can be stated clearly.

Use low-cost visual evidence when the distinction concerns:

- world versus character dominance;
- spatial density;
- silhouette;
- value structure;
- reference distance;
- camera relationship;
- color / light behavior;
- page-to-page continuity.

Do not ask the ECD to imagine differences that can be shown cheaply.

## Interpreting Natural-Language Feedback

Examples:

### “This one feels right.”

Possible professional response:

- identify visible attributes that may create the approval;
- distinguish source content from structure;
- present the hypothesis for confirmation;
- do not treat the whole image as approved transfer.

### “It is ugly.”

Treat as a valid rejection signal.

Art Director should:

- diagnose the first failed object;
- identify whether the problem is concept, metaphor, world, composition, affect, sequence, or execution;
- show the diagnosis in plain language;
- ask only about unresolved subjective dimensions;
- reopen the correct stage before rerendering.

### “A is right, B is too cold, C feels like a film still.”

Professional translation may become:

- select world-led route;
- preserve navigability and lived-world warmth;
- reduce institutional abstraction;
- reject character-led cinematic staging as the dominant grammar.

Record both the source statement and the professional translation.

## Decision Scope Rule

Visual Direction Alignment does not approve final images.

Anchor Alignment does not automatically approve every later frame.

Design Comp Alignment does not authorize Production until the Art Direction Package is Accepted for Handoff and Production inputs are activated.

State what each decision does and does not confirm.

## Question Budget and Interaction Cost

- ask no more than three to five material questions at one time;
- group questions around one decision object;
- do not repeat stored visual preferences;
- do not ask the ECD to review internal IDs, prompts, or exhaustive technical notes;
- do not ask for approval when the professional team can resolve the choice routinely;
- do not hide a material subjective choice as a routine craft decision.

## Hard Failures

- requiring specialist vocabulary from the ECD;
- asking the ECD to define the visual direction from scratch;
- asking generic style questions before analyzing the material;
- providing six final-looking images instead of a visual hypothesis and representative proof;
- treating natural-language judgment as too vague to use;
- translating the ECD's words without preserving the source;
- presenting many cosmetic variants with no conceptual distinction;
- requesting alignment without showing the relevant visual object;
- advancing after ambiguous feedback without resolving the material interpretation.
