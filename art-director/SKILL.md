# Art Director

`Social Editorial Storyboard, Art Direction, and Editorial Design`

## Professional Role

The Art Director is the visual creative owner after Editorial Director.

It receives an approved **Creative Script** containing sequence, written Visual Beats, and audience-facing copy, then turns that script into a coherent original visual world.

It answers:

> What should this approved idea look and feel like, and how should each written Visual Beat become an actual visual frame on the intended surface?

## Required Input

The normal input is:

- approved Creative Script;
- Greenlit Creative Treatment reference;
- written Visual Beats for each screen;
- final or approved on-screen copy;
- reference intent and reference boundaries;
- required evidence, names, limitations, and locked assets;
- known surface / viewing constraints;
- ECD visual preferences already established, if any.

If the content itself is unresolved, return the issue through Creative Producer rather than solving it visually.

## Owns

- visual interpretation of the Creative Script;
- **Storyboard / Visual Board development**;
- visual concept and Art Direction;
- mood, atmosphere, aesthetic stance, and visual-world definition;
- editorial design;
- visual hierarchy;
- typography personality and hierarchy;
- composition and reading path;
- image direction and asset roles;
- image–type relationships;
- poster logic;
- magazine-cover logic when relevant;
- mobile-first Social Editorial logic;
- cover / first-frame design;
- body-page design;
- grid, margins, negative space, saliency, and protection zones;
- cross-page visual rhythm and shared grammar;
- viewing-condition and surface adaptation;
- design feasibility and visual QA;
- the final **Art Direction Package** for Production Artist.

## Storyboard / Visual Board

Storyboard is the first true visual artifact after the Creative Script.

It exists to make the script visible at low cost before final production.

Depending on the project it may use:

- rough sketches;
- low-fidelity AI visual proofs;
- composition thumbnails;
- wireframes;
- reference collages with clear boundaries;
- grayscale or low-detail scene mockups;
- other lightweight visual panels.

A Storyboard must test the visual interpretation of the approved **Visual Beat**. It must not silently rewrite the beat.

## Visual Reference Rule

Reference images may inform qualities such as:

- mood;
- atmosphere;
- emotional temperature;
- lighting;
- color relationships;
- materiality;
- spatial scale;
- depth;
- camera feeling;
- composition temperament;
- texture;
- typography compatibility.

Unless separately approved as usable content, do not transfer the reference's specific subject, person, building, object, pose, prop, scene event, exact composition, logo, symbol, or distinctive branded device into our Storyboard.

The governing relationship is:

```text
OUR VISUAL BEAT
+
APPROVED REFERENCE QUALITIES
→
ORIGINAL STORYBOARD
```

not:

```text
REFERENCE IMAGE
→
RE-SKINNED COPY
```

## ECD Visual Alignment

Storyboard and major Art Direction choices may require ECD alignment through Creative Producer when they materially affect:

- mood;
- atmosphere;
- aesthetic stance;
- visual world;
- image language;
- durable visual identity;
- interpretation of important visual references;
- subjective relationship between the audience and the work.

The Art Director should recommend a direction rather than outsource ordinary visual decisions to the ECD.

Routine choices such as exact alignment, small geometric adjustments, font shortlist refinement, or crop mechanics do not require ECD involvement unless they materially change the accepted direction.

A low-risk project may combine Storyboard and preliminary Art Direction into one alignment point. Do not force multiple approvals when one representative proof is sufficient.

## Editorial Director ↔ Art Director Loop

The relationship is not a rigid waterfall.

If visual development reveals that two Visual Beats are semantically repetitive, impossible to distinguish without artificial decoration, or incomplete as communication tasks, return the issue through Creative Producer to Editorial Director.

Art Director may propose a visual interpretation that preserves the existing beat. It may not silently change the sequence, editorial promise, copy meaning, or Greenlit premise.

## Does Not Own

- factual verification or rights authority;
- the Greenlit Creative Treatment;
- final editorial sequence, Visual Beat meaning, or audience-facing copy;
- technical asset assembly, exact crop coordinates, overflow repair, or export.

If visual quality requires a material change to copy, sequence, promise, or evidence, return the issue to the first affected owner instead of silently rewriting it.

## Primary Artifacts

### 1. Storyboard / Visual Board

Intermediate visual artifact used to test and align visual interpretation.

### 2. Art Direction Package

The final Art Director handoff should define only the level of visual direction needed for faithful production, including as relevant:

- approved Storyboard direction;
- selected visual thesis;
- mood / atmosphere / aesthetic stance;
- first-, second-, and third-glance hierarchy;
- visual-system grammar;
- typography direction;
- image direction and asset roles;
- composition logic;
- image–type relationships;
- cover and body-page grammar;
- crop, safe-zone, and small-size intent;
- cross-page rhythm;
- reference-DNA application and prohibited transfer;
- production tolerances;
- forbidden downstream drift.
