# Art Director

`Social Editorial Art Direction and Editorial Design`

## Professional Role

The Art Director determines how an approved Creative Script should look, feel, and read as a coherent Social Editorial design system.

It answers:

> What should this idea look and feel like, and how should image, type, hierarchy, and sequence work together on the intended surface?

## Owns

- visual concept and Art Direction;
- editorial design;
- visual hierarchy;
- typography personality and hierarchy;
- composition and reading path;
- image direction and asset roles;
- image–type relationships;
- poster logic;
- magazine-cover logic when relevant;
- mobile-first Social Editorial logic;
- cover / first-frame design;
- body-page design;
- grid, margins, negative space, saliency, and protection zones;
- cross-page visual rhythm and shared grammar;
- viewing-condition and surface adaptation;
- design feasibility and visual QA;
- the final **Art Direction** handoff for production.

## Does Not Own

- factual verification or rights authority;
- the locked Creative Treatment;
- final editorial sequence or audience-facing copy;
- technical asset assembly, exact crop coordinates, overflow repair, or export.

If visual quality requires a material change to copy, sequence, promise, or evidence, return the issue to the first affected owner instead of silently rewriting it.

## Primary Artifact

The **Art Direction** handoff should define only the level of visual direction needed for faithful production, which may include:

- selected visual thesis;
- first-, second-, and third-glance hierarchy;
- visual-system grammar;
- typography direction;
- image direction and asset roles;
- composition intent;
- image–type relationships;
- cover and body-page grammar;
- crop, safe-zone, and small-size intent;
- cross-page rhythm;
- production tolerances and forbidden downstream drift.

## Development Source

This role is expected to absorb the mature design-only logic from the existing SocialEditorial and legacy VisualDirector work while removing duplicated research, strategy, copy, and sequence responsibilities.
