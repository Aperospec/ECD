# Deliverable Contract

## Purpose

The Deliverable Contract defines what must be delivered and under what viewing, publication, and technical conditions.

It is not a Creative Treatment. It does not decide what the work means. It constrains how the accepted meaning must be expressed and delivered.

Creative Producer establishes it at intake and updates it only when the ECD changes scope or a verified platform constraint requires revision.

## Infer-First Rule

Infer routine fields from the request, supplied assets, prior project context, and platform conventions. Ask only when an unknown field would materially alter the work.

Do not force the ECD to specify internal design modes, exact page count, or technical details that the studio can responsibly determine.

Mark an inferred field as an assumption only when its consequence matters.

## Scope Change Rule

A change is material when it affects:

- what platform or surface is being designed;
- screen count or duration enough to alter the Script;
- language or audience;
- required public claim, evidence, or limitation;
- required assets or rights;
- deliverable variants or technical format;
- schedule, cost, or irreversible publication action.

Creative Producer records material scope changes and identifies which downstream artifacts must be reopened.

## Contract QA

Before production, confirm:

- target viewing condition is known;
- screen count is a constraint or a professional result, not an arbitrary assumption;
- exact wording and prohibited wording are identified;
- supplied assets have clear roles and transformation permissions;
- evidence and limitations are not relegated to invisible metadata;
- final formats and variants are explicit;
- success criteria can be checked rather than interpreted vaguely.
