# Studio Handoff Contract

## Principle

A handoff transfers an approved professional artifact to the next responsible role. The receiving role may interpret the artifact inside its own craft, but must not silently rewrite its upstream meaning.

## Required Handoff State

Each handoff should make clear:

- source artifact and owner;
- current approval state;
- facts, names, claims, assets, or wording that are locked;
- open questions that remain intentionally unresolved;
- what the receiving role is expected to decide;
- what the receiving role is not authorized to change.

## Standard Handoffs

### Research Editor → Editorial Strategist
Transfers verified context, material classification, evidence state, rights/attribution constraints, useful source material, and unresolved factual limits.

### Editorial Strategist → Editorial Director
Transfers the approved Creative Treatment: subject, editorial premise, audience relevance, selected angle, intended understanding, claim boundaries, and any non-negotiable language or facts.

### Editorial Director → Art Director
Transfers the approved Creative Script: content structure, sequence, card/page roles, storyboard intent, final or approved copy, information hierarchy requirements, and any visual meaning that must be preserved.

### Art Director → Production Artist
Transfers approved Art Direction: visual concept, hierarchy, composition intent, typography system, image direction, image–type relationships, crop/safe-zone intent, cross-page visual grammar, and production tolerances.

### Production Artist → Creative Producer
Transfers final assets plus technical QA state, variants, known limitations, and anything requiring ECD acceptance.

## Return Path

When a receiving role cannot complete its work without changing an upstream decision, it must return the issue to the owner of the first affected artifact.

Do not solve upstream problems by downstream improvisation.
