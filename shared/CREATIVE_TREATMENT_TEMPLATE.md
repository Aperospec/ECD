# Creative Treatment Template

## Purpose

The Creative Treatment is the authoritative Development proposal that defines what the project is and why it deserves to enter Production.

It is first an **ECD-facing creative proposition** and only second a Development decision record.

For raw or unresolved input, the Treatment must be presented to the ECD and accepted before Production. The response presenting it ends at the Greenlight request.

Apply:

- `CREATIVE_TREATMENT_PRESENTATION.md` for visible hierarchy and narrative quality;
- `STAGE_SCOPED_INPUT_REGISTER.md` when the original brief spans multiple stages;
- `GREENLIGHT_RECORD.md` for authorization.

## Mandatory Visible Hierarchy

Every proposed Treatment must appear in this order:

1. **One-Sentence Creative Core**
2. **Creative Treatment / Creative Narrative**
3. **Supplemental Development Information**
4. **Deferred Input Notice**, when useful
5. **Greenlight Scope and Request**

The first two sections are mandatory and may not be displaced by metadata, boundaries, reference analysis, state records, or workflow explanation.

The ECD should understand the idea before reading how it is protected.

## 1. One-Sentence Creative Core

State in one complete sentence what the proposed work fundamentally does.

The sentence must:

- positively define the project;
- identify the central human material, transformation, or experience;
- be concrete enough to imagine;
- distinguish this treatment from a generic topic;
- function as an internal creative proposition rather than final audience-facing copy.

Do not begin with a list of exclusions or a defensive disclaimer.

## 2. Creative Treatment / Creative Narrative

Immediately explain the idea in coherent prose.

The narrative should make clear:

- what is happening;
- how the central material enters or changes through the work;
- what role the principal person, object, AI, product, or system plays;
- what is distinctive, moving, useful, or intellectually meaningful;
- what the audience should ultimately understand, feel, notice, imagine, or reconsider.

Use connected paragraphs, not a set of analytical labels that the ECD must assemble.

This section must remain Development-level. It may explain the governing situation and transformation without fixing final page count, page order, audience-facing copy, fictional biography, Storyboard, palette, camera, typography, composition, or layout.

## Internal Record Is Not the User-Facing Treatment

Creative Producer may maintain a detailed internal record containing premise diagnosis, evidence, uncertainty, reference classes, stage projections, rights, rejected options, and state metadata.

Do not expose that record at equal visual weight by default.

The ECD-facing Treatment is a synthesis. Surface only the supporting information required to decide the creative premise.

## Cross-Stage Boundary

A source statement may matter to Development and a later craft stage at the same time.

Example:

```text
ECD: “建筑非常宏大，人很小。”

Development meaning allowed in the Creative Narrative:
The work should make the scale and breadth of a person's life perceptible.

Deferred Art Direction input:
Monumental architecture with a small human figure.
```

Do not place the exact visual technique in a Greenlight lock unless the ECD explicitly made that technique a Development-level semantic condition.

A reference supplied for “色调、画风” may be registered during Development as reference intent, but Art Director performs detailed palette, lighting, material, and style extraction after Visual activation.

## Supplemental Development Information

After the creative core and narrative, include only the supplemental material required by the project:

- selected angle and core proposition, when these add precision;
- why the idea matters to the audience;
- reality, imagination, evidence, claim, rights, or attribution boundaries;
- concise semantic guardrails;
- material unresolved Development decisions;
- recommendation.

These sections must support the idea rather than replace it.

Avoid repeating the same boundary in several headings.

## Deferred Input Notice

When the raw brief contains important later-stage information, show a concise notice after the supplemental Development information and before Greenlight:

```markdown
## Deferred Inputs Recorded — Not Part of This Greenlight

Editorial:
- [source-faithful user direction; no final sequence interpretation]

Visual / Art Direction:
- [source-faithful visual preference or reference intent; no premature palette or composition extraction]

Production:
- [when relevant]
```

This notice proves that downstream information was captured. It is outside the authoritative Treatment body and is not accepted merely because the Treatment is Greenlit.

Do not populate it with assistant-invented downstream decisions.

## Presentation Rule

For raw or unresolved input:

1. decompose the full brief in the Stage-Scoped Input Register;
2. activate only Development projections;
3. write the One-Sentence Creative Core;
4. write the complete Creative Narrative immediately after it;
5. verify the ECD can understand the project from those two sections alone;
6. add only necessary supplemental Development information;
7. optionally show a concise, source-faithful Deferred Input Notice;
8. state the recommendation and exact Greenlight consequence;
9. ask one concise decision question;
10. end the response.

Do not continue into formal scripting or visual production in the same response.

A later ECD reply may be concise when it clearly refers to this Treatment. Record the authorization under `GREENLIGHT_RECORD.md`.

The only exceptions to a new presentation turn are:

- an identifiable prior Greenlight;
- a stage-aware ECD instruction explicitly identifying supplied material as the final / approved Treatment and authorizing Production from it.

Generic requests to make, start, design, or directly produce the deliverable are not exceptions.

## Creative Treatment Narrative Gate

Before submitting for Greenlight, confirm:

- the first substantive section is a one-sentence creative core;
- the second section is a coherent prose Creative Treatment;
- the ECD can explain the project without reading later guardrails;
- the Treatment states what the work is before emphasizing what it is not;
- the central idea is not scattered across analytical headings;
- internal state, risk, evidence, reference, and workflow notes are subordinate;
- the topic has become a specific project premise;
- audience relevance and intended consequence are concrete;
- reality, speculation, evidence, and claim limits remain clear;
- cross-stage statements were projected rather than flattened into the Treatment;
- later-stage user instructions remain Deferred and preserved;
- Assistant Inference remains distinguishable from user-supplied constraints;
- `Must preserve` contains Development-level meaning rather than unactivated visual technique;
- the document has not drifted into final sequence, copy deck, Storyboard, palette, camera, typography, composition, or layout;
- the Greenlight request authorizes Creative Script development next rather than immediate visual production;
- the response ends at the ECD decision.

The Treatment fails when the ECD reasonably has to ask:

> “你真正想表达的内容到底在哪里？”

When that happens:

1. identify the missing creative core and narrative;
2. revise the complete Treatment as a new version;
3. mark the prior version Superseded;
4. place the corrected core and narrative first;
5. resubmit the revised Treatment for Greenlight.

Do not leave the deficient Treatment active and provide the actual idea only as an informal follow-up summary.
