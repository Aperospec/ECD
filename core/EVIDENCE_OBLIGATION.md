# Evidence Obligation — V3.1

Constitution clauses: `SEM-011`–`SEM-013`, `SEM-017`.

## Purpose

Evidence depth follows the promise the work makes.

A conceptual image, an editorial explanation based on a public demonstration, and a productized workflow offered with a reliability promise are not held to the same proof standard.

Creative Producer records one primary Evidence Obligation during initialization. Individual claims may receive a stricter local obligation.

## Modes

### 1. Speculative / Conceptual

Use when the work is primarily:

- imagination;
- metaphor;
- hypothetical example;
- fictional scene;
- concept visualization;
- speculative future;
- creative exploration that does not claim current feasibility or observed performance.

Required behavior:

- preserve an explicit conceptual, hypothetical, fictional, or speculative position in the main communication;
- verify only external factual premises that materially affect meaning;
- do not demand product testing merely because the concept is futuristic;
- distinguish source inspiration from the studio's own concept;
- record rights, attribution, and direct-use limits for third-party material;
- label reconstruction, simulation, or concept visualization where a reasonable viewer might mistake it for evidence.

Claim ceiling:

- may state what the work imagines, explores, reframes, or proposes;
- may not imply that the depicted system currently exists, works reliably, or was personally experienced unless separately verified.

### 2. Evidence-based Editorial

Use when publication relies on a real:

- post, demonstration, repository, paper, product, event, dataset, screenshot, interface, news item, or public claim.

Required behavior:

- inspect the relevant original or primary source where available;
- verify the key facts the public communication depends on;
- separate source claim, observed demonstration, independently verified fact, inference, and interpretation;
- record date, version, region, availability, sample, or other material limits;
- preserve context needed to interpret evidence correctly;
- keep evidence inspectable in Editorial and Visual work;
- do not escalate automatically into complete product validation when the publication makes only a narrow editorial claim.

Claim ceiling:

- public language may be no stronger than the verified source and observed evidence;
- demonstration is not proof of general reliability, availability, safety, or commercial readiness.

### 3. Product / Commercial Validation

Use when the studio intends to:

- sell or productize a Skill, workflow, product, or service;
- recommend it as reliably usable;
- make a concrete performance, repeatability, compatibility, cost, or operational promise;
- support a purchase, deployment, or other consequential decision.

Required behavior, as applicable:

- validate setup and execution on representative inputs;
- record environment, dependencies, version, access, region, and tool requirements;
- test reproducibility rather than relying on one successful example;
- document failure cases, boundaries, and recovery conditions;
- distinguish upstream creator claims from observed studio results;
- identify cost, time, operational, privacy, security, rights, license, and redistribution constraints;
- define what the offer includes and excludes;
- narrow the public promise when evidence is insufficient.

Claim ceiling:

- a commercial or reliability claim may not exceed observed and reproducible evidence;
- when practical validation cannot be performed, the system must disclose the limitation and may not relabel source claims as validated performance.

## Hybrid projects and local overrides

A project has one primary Evidence Obligation, but individual statements may require a stricter mode.

Examples of valid structure:

- a speculative visual concept built around one current factual premise: primary `Speculative / Conceptual`, with the premise verified as `Evidence-based Editorial`;
- an editorial post that later proposes selling a workflow: editorial claims use `Evidence-based Editorial`; the future offer remains unvalidated until `Product / Commercial Validation` is completed.

The stricter obligation governs the affected claim, not necessarily every part of the project.

## Selection test

Producer and Development Director answer:

1. What would a reasonable audience believe after seeing this work?
2. Does the work claim that something is true now, was actually experienced, or works reliably?
3. Is the audience being asked to buy, deploy, trust, or act on a capability?
4. What evidence would be necessary if the claim were challenged?
5. What is the consequence of being wrong?

Choose the lowest mode that fully supports the actual promise. Do not under-validate for speed or over-validate for ceremony.

## Stage consequences

### Development

- records primary mode and claim-level overrides;
- commissions Research Verification and Claims Rights to the depth required;
- limits the Treatment to supportable fact, concept, or commercial position;
- records validation gaps as boundaries or blockers.

### Editorial

- preserves certainty, speaker position, source context, qualification, attribution, and disclosure;
- places material limitations where they affect interpretation;
- does not convert a conceptual or source-claimed result into personal fact or validated capability.

### Visual

- distinguishes evidence, reconstruction, simulation, and concept imagery;
- keeps material proof inspectable;
- does not use visual realism to imply a stronger reality status than the evidence supports.

### Production

- preserves evidence labels, source context, qualifiers, and required disclosures;
- does not crop, retouch, or redesign evidence into a materially different claim.

## Research and validation sufficiency

Research is sufficient when it answers the decision the project actually needs and constrains the public promise accordingly.

Product / Commercial Validation is sufficient only when:

- representative execution or a documented substitute exists;
- reproducibility and failure boundaries are known to the degree promised;
- dependencies and operational conditions are stated;
- rights and commercial-use conditions are understood;
- public language matches the evidence.

A source article, creator video, or one attractive output alone is not product validation.

## Failure routing

- incorrect mode or promise → Creative Producer / Development Director;
- missing or stale fact → Research Verification;
- source use, license, attribution, likeness, or implied endorsement → Claims Rights;
- evidence changes the premise → Concept Development;
- public copy exceeds evidence → Copywriting / Copy Editing;
- visual proof implies unsupported reality → Visual Department;
- validation cannot be performed with available capabilities → Blocked or narrower claim, recorded by Producer.

## Quality gate

Pass only when:

- the Evidence Obligation is explicit;
- research depth is proportionate;
- claim-level overrides are recorded;
- fact, source claim, inference, concept, demonstration, simulation, and validated result remain distinct;
- the public claim ceiling is usable downstream;
- unresolved validation is disclosed rather than silently assumed.
